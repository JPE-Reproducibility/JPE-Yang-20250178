      Overview 
      The code in this replication package reproduces every table and figure in the paper and its 
      appendix — 57 exhibits — from raw data, in two stages: 
       • Stage A (code/generate/ , 25 programs organized in nine dependency-ordered 
         steps) rebuilds every analysis dataset from the raw extracts. The package ships raw 
         data only:  confidential-data-not-for-publication/Analysis/  is empty at 
         delivery and is populated entirely by Stage A. 
       • Stage B (code/tables/ ,  code/figures/ ) reproduces every exhibit from the Stage-A 
         datasets with one self-contained script per exhibit, writing to  output/tables/  and 
         output/figures/ . 
      One command runs everything:  bash code/run_all.sh . The replicator should expect 
      about 1.5–2 hours for the main analysis (Stage-A steps 01–04 plus Tables 2–8 / Figures 2–4), 
      and roughly 8–10 hours of additional compute for the full appendix, dominated by the 
      Table A8 Monte-Carlo simulation (≈5–6 hours on 7 parallel Stata workers) and two long 
      placebo/robustness builds. A  --skip-heavy  switch skips the two longest builds. 
      Data fall into two folders, split by redistribution rights: 
       • data/raw/  — all publicly redistributable raw inputs (World Bank WDI, USPTO 
         PatentsView bulk files, Natural Earth, SimpleMaps/GeoNames gazetteers, UN 
         voting ideal points, Polity5, UN Comtrade extract, and every author-compiled 
         classification file). This folder is part of the public deposit. 
       • confidential-data-not-for-publication/  — the proprietary inputs (PitchBook 
         extracts; Capital IQ, State Street, Hurun, and Zero2IPO workbooks; and machine-
         learning outputs derived from PitchBook/PATSTAT text), provided to the JPE Data 
         Editor under the journal's confidential-data exemption, for the sole purpose of the 
         reproducibility checks. This folder is excluded from the public deposit. 
      The one sanctioned exception to "everything from raw" is a set of nine frozen machine-
      learning outputs (BERT sector classifications and SBERT text-similarity measures) that are          too computationally intensive to regenerate in the pipeline and are not bit-reproducible 
         (GPU training). The complete original code that produced them, with a file-by-file 
         provenance map, ships in  code/BERT_generation/  (see its  README.md  and  PROVENANCE.md ).

        PitchBook (confidential) 
        The core data are four extracts from the PitchBook Platform (PitchBook Data, Inc.), 
        covering global venture-capital deals, companies, investors, and the deal–investor link 
        table, plus the analyst "market map" taxonomy ( pbmarketmap_v2.csv : companyid × 
        marketmap|segment|subsegment with company descriptions), a 2022–24 refresh 
        (later_years_extension/ ), and a fund–limited- partner extract ( LP.dta ). Format: 
        Stata  .dta  /  .csv ; the variable lists for all PitchBook files are reproduced in full in the Data 
        Appendix of this README. The extracts were downloaded by the authors through Harvard 
        Business School's institutional PitchBook subscription (2023 baseline; the 2022–24 refresh 
        pulled in 2025). PitchBook data are proprietary and may not be redistributed; researchers 
        can obtain access through an institutional or commercial PitchBook license 
        (https://pitchbook.com; academic licensing is negotiated with PitchBook's institutional 
        sales — there is no posted price). The files are provided to the JPE Data Editor under the 
        journal's confidential-data exemption, to be destroyed after the reproducibility checks; 
        they are excluded from the public deposit. The authors will assist with reasonable 
        replication attempts for ten years following publication. 
        Machine-learning outputs derived from PitchBook and PATSTAT text (confidential) 
        confidential-data-not-for-publication/Raw/BERT_prediction_resource/  holds the 
        nine frozen outputs of the paper's three expensive ML layers: (1) BERT market-map sector 
        classification of every PitchBook company description 
        (company_level_predictions_v2.dta ); (2) SBERT business-description similarity to 
        Chinese and U.S. firms ( similarity_compiled.csv ,  similarity_compiled_western.csv , 
        similarity_at_sector_x_country_x_year.dta ); (3) patent-text sector labels and 
        similarity ( patent_sector_labels_{grantyear,new_grantyear,new100k}.dta , 
        patent_countrysector_similarity_2000_2013_100k_new_china.dta , 
        worldwide_pairs_filtered_no_EPO_countries.dta  — the last derived from EPO PATSTAT 
        family abstracts). Because they embed PitchBook text (and, for the last file, PATSTAT-
        licensed inputs), they are confidential. Every file's producing code, model, 
        hyperparameters, inputs, and compute cost are documented in  code/BERT_generation/  
        (original author code plus  PROVENANCE.md ); regenerating them is optional and not bit-
        reproducible (GPU training). Every  .dta  file ships with a CSV twin (see Dataset list). 
        Capital IQ (confidential) 
        Figure 1 Panel C compares IPO counts from S&P Global's Capital IQ platform with 
        PitchBook-based counts. Three author-built tabulation workbooks 
        (figure1c_IPO_CapitalIQ_to_PB_{EM,US,nonEM_nonUS}*.xlsx ) contain the Capital IQ 
        screens and the mapping to the paper's country groups. The non-EM/non-US workbook 
        additionally embeds the authors' manual, research-based corrections of the Capital IQ 
        output (the case-by-case adjustments described in the paper's appendix); these hand 
        edits are part of the shipped workbook and are not mechanically reproducible. Capital IQ          is proprietary (https://www.capitaliq.spglobal.com; institutional subscription); the 
         workbooks are provided to the Data Editor only. 
         State Street (confidential) 
         Table 1 Panel C reports pooled internal rates of return and Kaplan–Schoar (2005) public-
         market-equivalents for venture-capital funds by regional focus, over 2007Q3–2019Q4. 
         State Street Corporation, one of the world's largest custodians (roughly $49 trillion in 
         assets under custody and/or administration as of June 30, 2025), computes these returns 
         in its custodial role, maintaining a comprehensive record of all investor–GP cash flows (net 
         of management fees and carried interest) across VC, private equity, real estate, hedge 
         funds, securitizations, and other alternative assets. The underlying universe spans 142 
         large asset owners with VC and PE exposure, collectively over 120,000 investments, and 
         4,008 private-capital funds (vintages 1988–2024) covering VC (early-stage, balanced, and 
         late-stage), buyout, and private-debt strategies (distressed, mezzanine, and special 
         lending); State Street classifies funds by type from documents provided by the fund 
         managers and asset owners. Panel C tabulates only the venture-capital funds, broken out 
         by the region each specialized in; funds without a clear geographic focus, or spanning 
         multiple regions, are excluded from the tabulation. The data were provided by Nan Zhang 
         (Head of Product Development and Managing Director, State Street Data Intelligence) and 
         pulled from the database on June 24, 2025; see Lerner, Mao, Schoar and Zhang (2022) for a 
         detailed discussion and validation of these data. The provider summary workbook 
         (table1_panelC_...xlsx ) is not redistributable and has no public distribution channel; 
         access is negotiated with State Street's Data Intelligence group. Provided to the Data 
         Editor only. 
         Hurun China Rich List (confidential) 
         Table 1 Panel D's founder counts are hand-tabulated from the Hurun China Rich List 2020 
         (Hurun Research Institute, https://www.hurun.net), matched by the authors to PitchBook 
         company records to establish VC backing. The tabulation workbook 
         (table1_panelD_Chinese VC.xlsx ) is provided to the Data Editor only. 
         Zero2IPO (confidential) 

         Figure A4 cross-validates PitchBook's China venture series against Zero2IPO (清科) data 
         obtained through the Harvard Business School China Research Center. Zero2IPO data are 
         licensed (https://www.pedata.cn); the two comparison workbooks are provided to the 
         Data Editor only. RMB values are converted to USD in code. 
         World Bank World Development Indicators (public; provided) 
         The appropriateness/suitability measure is built from the World Development Indicators. 
         data/raw/suitability_resource/WDI_full.xlsx  is the pinned bulk extract used by the 
         paper (all countries × indicators × 1960–2021, with WDI series codes). WDI data are CC-BY 
         4.0 and can be re-downloaded at https://databank.worldbank.org/source/world-
         development-indicators (choose "All countries", "All series", "All years", Excel) — note that          the World Bank revises the database continuously (including retroactive revisions and 
         indicator retirements), so an exact re-download of this vintage is not possible; the pinned 
         extract ships instead. The four author-built indicator→sector assignment maps ship in the 
         same folder ( indicators_WDI_MtV_*.xlsx ; the  corrected  map is the one used by the 
         paper, the other three feed the Table A4 assignment-robustness comparison). The 
         assignment of WDI indicators to the fifteen market-map sectors is likewise the authors' 
         own manual classification (the gpt_assigned variant, which enters only the Table A4 
         comparison, was instead produced with a large language model). 
         USPTO PatentsView (public; provided) 
         The entire patent layer is rebuilt from seven raw PatentsView bulk TSVs ( g_patent , 
         g_us_patent_citation ,  g_cpc_current ,  g_application ,  g_assignee_disambiguated , 
         g_location_disambiguated ,  g_inventor_disambiguated ; 21 GB total). PatentsView 
         (https://patentsview.org, downloads at https://patentsview.org/download/data-download-
         tables) is free, CC-BY 4.0, no registration required. PatentsView publishes quarterly 
         releases and does not archive superseded vintages, so the package ships the authors' 
         pinned copies (the CPC table is the February 2023 release; the citation table is the 
         November 2023 release; the remaining tables were downloaded in 2023–24). A newer 
         release would shift disambiguation and therefore downstream counts; the shipped vintage 
         reproduces the paper. 
         Natural Earth; SimpleMaps; OpenDataSoft/GeoNames (public; provided) 
         Company and patent geolocation uses: Natural Earth 1:10m Admin-0 country boundaries 
         and Populated Places (public domain, https://www.naturalearthdata.com); the 
         SimpleMaps World Cities Database, Basic edition (CC-BY 4.0, 
         https://simplemaps.com/data/world-cities); and the "All cities with a population > 1000" 
         GeoNames extract republished by OpenDataSoft (CC-BY 4.0, 
         https://public.opendatasoft.com/explore/dataset/geonames-all-cities-with-a-population-
         1000/). All are shipped in  data/raw/geocoding_resource/ , along with the authors' 
         country/state name crosswalk ( crosswalk.xlsx ). 
         UN General Assembly ideal points and Polity5 (public; provided) 
         The political-proximity control uses dynamic UN-voting ideal points from Bailey, Strezhnev 
         and Voeten (2017), obtained from the paper's public replication data 
         (https://doi.org/10.7910/DVN/LEJUQZ), and regime scores from the Polity5 Annual Time-
         Series 1946–2018 (Center for Systemic Peace, free download at 
         https://www.systemicpeace.org/inscrdata.html). The two are shipped combined in one 
         country–year panel assembled for the authors' earlier work 
         (data/raw/other_data_resource/un_polity_china.dta , with CSV twin), carrying each 
         country's values alongside China's. The distance-to-China controls are computed in code 
         (code/generate/03b_generate_controls.py ):  un_distance = |un_ideal_point − 
         china_un_ideal_point|  and  polity_distance = |polity_score −           china_polity_score| . Coverage note: Polity reaches back before the UN series (China's 
          ideal points begin 1971), so early years have missing UN values. 

          UN Comtrade (public; provided) 

          The China trade-share control uses a UN Comtrade extract 
          (Trade_Volume_Export_Import.csv : annual total-trade flows with China, in the standard 
          Comtrade bulk column layout). Comtrade data are free at https://comtradeplus.un.org 
          (registration optional for small queries). The extract ships in the package. 

          Government vs. private Chinese VC (public; provided) 

          Table 1 Panel B's classification of Chinese VC funding by government/private source is 
          tabulated from the data of Beraja, Peng, Yang and Yuchtman (2024). The tabulation 
          (table1_panelB_funding_sources.csv , 3 columns) ships in the package; the underlying 
          data are available with that paper. 

          Global Innovation Measures country dataset (author-compiled; supplemented; 
          confidential) 

          Table A1 and Figure A1 draw on an author-compiled country–year workbook 
          (CountryDataset.2024019.supplemented.xlsx; confidential folder) combining VC activity, 
          GDP per capita, R&D spending, scientific publications, and U.S. patents by country, 1969–
          2021. The workbook is the authors' own compilation, assembled by hand rather than by 
          any program in the package: the underlying series were collected from the sources 
          described next and cleaned and harmonized directly in the workbook. The VC columns are 
          consolidated from the authors' PitchBook Platform pulls (deal counts and amounts by 
          country and year, from the same platform extracts as the core data). The publications 
          series (1996 onward) is taken directly from NSF Science and Engineering Indicators 
          country counts; the only change is renaming entries to a standard country-naming 
          convention used across the measures (the 1973–1995 counts are the Web of Science 
          supplement described below). The R&D series splices three sources — OECD (1981–1995), 
          World Bank (1996–2014), and UNESCO (2015–2021) — with judgmental choices between 
          overlapping vintages; short interior gaps are linearly interpolated and series ends are 
          carried forward, but values are never back-filled before a country's first observation. 
          Because of the judgment involved, the package ships the compiled workbook (all cleaning 
          sheets included, documenting every source series and imputation) plus the exhibit code, 
          rather than a from-scratch reconstruction. CountryDataset.2024019.supplemented.xlsx is 
          the authors’ compiled Global Innovation Measures workbook (CountryDataset.2024019) 
          with three documented supplements added for replication: (1) pre-1996 publication 
          counts inserted from a Science Citation Index (Web of Science) country-count extract, 
          1973–1995 (compiled by Kathleen Ryan, Baker Research & Data Services, accessed 
          6/5/2023) — the reason the workbook is confidential; (2) explicit zeros for three pre-
          coverage VC cells used by Table A1 (Israel 1969, Singapore 1979, Chile 1993; no recorded 
          deals); (3) two imputed R&D levels carried from the authors’ original hand tabulation, 
          South Korea 1988 (2.0% of GDP) and Chile 1993 (0.78% of GDP) — assumptions, not          source data. With these supplements, Table A1 is derived entirely from this workbook plus 
         tableA1_VCpc.xlsx, and the per-cell source of every Table A1 entry is recorded in 
         output/intermediate/tableA1_source_audit.csv. tableA1_published_values.csv is an 
         author transcription of Table A1 as printed in the manuscript; it no longer 
         supplies any table value — table_A1.py takes from it only the country list, 
         the emergence years, and the print-N/A gating for the VC-per-capita column, 
         and recomputes every entry from the workbook ;  tableA1_VCpc.xlsx  (VC per capita, 
         PitchBook-derived) is in the confidential folder. 
         Curated global venture-investment series (author-compiled; confidential) 
         Global VC_0525.xlsx (confidential folder) is the authors' curated compilation of published 
         venture-investment tabulations: annual VC invested, in current US$ billions, for the US, 
         China, Western Europe, Canada, Japan, Australia, a developing-country residual, and the 
         world, 2001–2022. The 2001–2018 base is the National Science Board's Science and 
         Engineering Indicators 2020, Table S8-62 (itself PitchBook-sourced); the 2019–22 values 
         are extended region by region from the NVCA Yearbook 2023 (US and world), the authors' 
         PitchBook analysis and PitchBook-sourced press reporting (China), and Invest Europe, 
         CVCA, Initial Enterprise, and Cut Through Venture (other regions), converted at IRS yearly-
         average exchange rates; every source and adjustment is noted in the workbook itself. No 
         exhibit or program reads this file: it is the quoted source for the manuscript's 2021 world 
         venture-investment total (opening Background paragraph); all other in-text numbers are 
         cells of the exhibits or immediate arithmetic on them. Provided to the Data Editor only. 
         Hand-collected classification files (author-compiled; provided) 

         data/raw/other_data_resource/hand_collected/  contains nine author files — four 
         classification .dta files with CSV twins plus one .xlsx (full variable lists in the Data 
         Appendix): OECD membership and join years ( OECD_list.dta ; the paper's EM definition is 
         non-OECD as of 1980); sector policy-constraint codings hand-linked from public policy 
         documents ( EM_policy_constraints.dta ,  policy_constrained_sectors.dta ); 
         critical/state-supported technology flags mapping "Made in China 2025", critical-supply-
         chain, and U.S. DoD critical-technology lists to PitchBook subsegments 
         (critical_subseg.dta); and the tradable-sector classification behind Table 6/A16 and Figure 
         A16 (table6_A16_Tradable or not 2nd.xlsx). The Table A1 published-values CSV, the Panel 
         B funding-shares tabulation documented above, and the country panel of bilateral 
         gravity/distance measures relative to China (country_panel.dta + CSV twin, assembled 
         from standard gravity sources; see the paper's Data Appendix) sit at the 
         other_data_resource/ folder root; the supplemented Global Innovation Measures 
         workbook is in the confidential folder (see above). The deal-type → category classification 
         for the non-VC analyses is hardcoded in 
         code/generate/05c_generate_regression_alltype_by_cat.do (formerly a spreadsheet).          EPO PATSTAT (upstream; not shipped)

         Statistical Database (PATSTAT Global; licensed product of the European Patent Office, 
         https://www.epo.org/en/searching-for-patents/business/patstat — annual subscription, 
         on the order of €1,250/year for the Global edition). PATSTAT itself is not shipped; only the

         Description of programs/code 
            •  code/auxiliary/paths.do  /  paths.py  define every path relative to the package root; 
               no machine-specific path exists anywhere in the code. Every program locates 
               the root itself by walking up the directory tree to  code/auxiliary/paths.do , so 
               programs can be run from the package root or any folder inside it (or set the 
               environment variable  REPLICATION_ROOT  /  JPE_GENERATE_ROOT  explicitly). Public 
               raw data are read from  data/raw/ ; confidential raw data from  confidential-data-
               not-for-publication/Raw/ ; generated data are written under  confidential-data-
               not-for-publication/Analysis/  (they are PitchBook-derived and therefore 
               confidential). The path setup recreates  Analysis/  and the  output/  tree if absent. 
            •  code/auxiliary/install.do  installs the required Stata community packages (run 
               once).  code/auxiliary/scheme-eop.scheme  is the graph scheme. 
            •  code/generate/  — Stage A: 25 programs in nine dependency-ordered steps (driver: 
               generate_all.sh , which accepts  --from NN --to NN  step ranges and  --skip-
               heavy ): 
                 o  01 builds the deal/company base  analysis_v2.dta  from the PitchBook 
                    extracts + BERT sector predictions + SBERT similarity + OECD list. 
                 o  02 builds the appropriateness (suitability) score family from the WDI extract 
                    and the indicator→sector maps (paper measure plus all-country, alternative-
                    assignment, and placebo variants). 
                 o  03a converts the Natural Earth populated-places shapefile to  .dta ; 03b 
                    builds the country-year controls (internet penetration, China trade/FDI 
                    shares, UN/Polity distances); 03c builds regression auxiliaries (investor-
                    origin flags, LP shares from  LP.dta , the shock-year file, serial-founder 
                    inputs).                  o  04 assembles the keystone regression panel 
                    regression_corrected_120623.dta  (sector×country×year). After 01–04, 
                    Tables 2–8 and Figures 2–4 reproduce. The keystone file is never modified 
                    by any later step. 
                 o  05a–05i build robustness/extension inputs: post-regression inputs (05a), the 
                    GDP-suitability component (05b), deal-type-by-category and cluster panels 
                    (05c, 05d), pre-period deals (05e), western-split similarity (05f), the 
                    patent/PATSTAT validation panel for Table A5 (05g), and the 2022–24 
                    extension (05h, 05i). 
                 o  06a streams the raw PatentsView citation table into located-citation files; 
                    06b builds the patent sector layers; 06c builds a patent-augmented copy of 
                    the keystone ( regression_corrected_120623_patents.dta , read only by 
                    Table A6). 
                 o  07a–07d build CPC percentile flags, patent geolocation, company 
                    geolocation, and the city-level panel ( analysis_city ). 
                 o  08a re-estimates the Table A13 specification variants; 08b builds the figure-
                    A11 dropped-indicator placebo stacks. 
                 o  09 runs the Table A8 Monte-Carlo simulation (500 draws × 2 designs; parallel 
                    driver  09_generate_simulated_deals.sh <workers> ). 
            •  code/tables/ ,  code/figures/  — Stage B: one self-contained program per exhibit 
               (table_1.py  …  table_A30.do ,  figure_1.py  …  figure_A17.do ;  .py  for the 
               workbook-based descriptive exhibits,  .do  otherwise). Each program reads only 
               Stage-A datasets and/or raw workbooks and writes its exhibit to  output/ . 
            •  code/BERT_generation/  — the original author code for the frozen ML layer, with a 
               file-by-file provenance map ( PROVENANCE.md ): the BERT company classifier, the 
               SBERT similarity pipeline, the patent classifiers (including the SLURM setup used for 
               full-corpus inference), and a verified reconstruction program for the three 
               patent_sector_labels_*  files. Rerunning is optional and not bit-reproducible (GPU 
               training); the frozen outputs are the paper's numbers. 
            •  code/run_all.sh  — master driver: Stage A, then every Stage-B exhibit in order (flags: 
               --skip-heavy ,  --stage-a-only ,  --stage-b-only ). 
         One dataset-level note: the patent layer is rebuilt entirely from the raw PatentsView TSVs 
         — located citations, citation counts, CPC percentile flags, geolocation — with zero frozen 
         patent inputs except the BERT/SBERT text measures. The shipped citation table is the only 
         surviving vintage; it postdates the one used for the paper's  num_citations  variable, which 
         shifts ~80k cells of  analysis_city  in columns no regression uses — Tables A29/A30 and 
         Figure A17 are unaffected (verified byte-identical from either vintage).

          Confidential files (variable lists) 

          pbdeals.dta  (95 variables) — one row per deal. Key variables used by the pipeline: 
          companyid ,  companyname ,  dealid ,  dealno ,  dealdate ,  dealsize  (USD m),  dealstatus , 
          dealtype /dealtype2 /dealtype3 ,  dealclass ,  premoneyvaluation ,  postvaluation , 
          raisedtodate ,  vcround ,  totalinvestedcapital ,  investors ,  newinvestors , 
          followoninvestors ,  nativecurrencyofdeal ,  businessstatus ,  financingstatus , plus IPO 
          fields ( tickersymbol ,  exchange ,  marketcapendoffirsttradingday , …), deal-term fields 
          (typeofstock ,  liquidationpreferences ,  boardvotingrights , …), and company financials 
          at deal time ( revenue ,  ebitda ,  netincome , valuation multiples). All 95 names follow 
          PitchBook's deal-report field names, lowercased, with the original PitchBook names 
          preserved as Stata variable labels inside the file. 

          pbcompanies.dta  (94 variables) — one row per company: identifiers and names 
          (companyid ,  companyname ,  companylegalname , …), the free-text  description  and  keywords  
          used by the ML layers,  yearfounded , the HQ location block ( hqlocation ,  hqcity , 
          hqstate_province ,  hqcountry ,  hqglobalregion , …), financing summary 
          (firstfinancingdate /size /dealtype  …,  lastfinancing* ,  lastknownvaluation* , 
          totalraised ), investor lists ( activeinvestors ,  formerinvestors ), and PitchBook's 
          growth/web/social metrics. 

          pbinvestors.dta  (94 variables) — one row per investor:  investorid ,  investorname , 
          description_inv ,  primaryinvestortype_inv ,  otherinvestortypes_inv ,  aum_inv ,           yearfounded_inv , the HQ block ( hqcountry_inv , …), activity counts 
          (totalinvestments_inv ,  totalexits_inv , …), fund counts, and stated investment 
          preferences ( preferred*_inv ). 

          pbdealinvestor.dta  (11 variables) — deal–investor links:  dealid ,  investorid , 
          investorname ,  investorstatus ,  isleadinvestor ,  investorfundid ,  investorfundname , 
          investorwebsite ,  leadpartnerid ,  leadpartnername ,  lastupdated .

          Vintage ,  CloseDate ,  FundLocation ,  FundCity ,  FundStatus ,  FundSize ,

          marketmap# /segment# /subsegment#  splits.
